THE PROBLEM

Three access gaps. One sensitive change with no gate.

The case shows overlapping weaknesses across integration access, Finance files and offboarding. They are important signals to contain; the evidence does not prove one actor connected them.

01

TallyBridge access was broad

The integration key was used for unusual exports and later for a successful vendor bank-detail update.

CASE SIGNAL E01 · E04 · E18
02

BlueVault key was exposed

A support-ticket key was used to read finance files. Its unsynchronized clock leaves cross-system timing uncertain.

CASE SIGNAL E05 · E13
03

Offboarding did not close access

Rohan’s account authenticated after his termination date. The log does not establish who used it.

CASE SIGNAL E03 · E14

The gap Orbit addresses: there was no reliable gate to stop or hold a sensitive bank change when identity, vendor permission, approval or risk checks needed review.

WHAT ORBIT CAN STOP

Check the request
before it takes effect.

Each sensitive change passes through four checks. If any one fails, Orbit holds it for a reviewer.

Failed checkHold · Alert · Preserve evidence
01

Identity and credential

Check that the user or integration is active, and that its credential is restricted and valid for this use.

ACTIVE · RESTRICTED · VALID
02

Permission for this vendor

Check the exact vendor and field. A contact-sync integration should not be able to edit a bank account.

OBJECT-LEVEL ACCESS
03

Approval for these details

Match Finance approval to this vendor and the exact new bank details—not just a generic approval number.

VENDOR + ACCOUNT
04

Risk context

Review source, client version and related activity. Uncertain clocks stay marked uncertain; context raises review, not a verdict.
